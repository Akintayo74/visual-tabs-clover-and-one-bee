# Installing Visual Tabs

Visual Tabs saves a website exactly as it looks, and your new tab becomes a wall of everything
you've saved. You find a site again by describing what you remember about it.

This takes about five minutes. You need Google Chrome on a computer.

## 1. Install it

1. Unzip the file you were sent (`visual-tabs-….zip`) into a folder you'll keep, for example
   **Documents › Visual Tabs**. Chrome runs the extension from this folder, so don't delete or
   move it afterwards.
2. In Chrome, go to `chrome://extensions` (type it in the address bar).
3. Turn on **Developer mode** (top right).
4. Click **Load unpacked** and choose the folder from step 1 (the one that contains
   `manifest.json`).
5. Click the puzzle-piece icon to the right of the address bar and pin **Visual Tabs**, so its
   button is always in the toolbar.
6. Open a new tab. Chrome asks whether to keep the new-tab page this extension added: choose
   **Keep it**.

If Chrome ever suggests turning off extensions in developer mode, choose to keep Visual Tabs.

## 2. Turn on backup

On the new tab, click **Sign in to back up** and enter your email. You'll get a 6-digit code;
type it in. From then on every save is backed up, and searching by description works. If you
use Chrome on another computer, install it there too and sign in with the same email: the same
wall appears.

## 3. Save sites

- **Alt+Shift+S** saves the page you're on. On a Mac, Alt is the **Option (⌥)** key.
- **Alt+Shift+X** saves it and closes the tab.
- Or click the toolbar button. Right-click it for **Save and close tab**.
- **Import open tabs** on the new tab saves every tab in the window at once. The window flips
  through your tabs while it works; when it's done you can close all the saved ones.

A small "Saved" message appears on the page. Saving a page again updates its screenshot.

If a shortcut does nothing, another extension may be using it: click **Help** on the new tab,
then **Change shortcuts**.

## 4. Find them again

Open a new tab and describe what you remember, the way you'd tell a friend: "the dark site with
the floating 3D thing", "a one-page site that's just big links". Quick matches appear as you
type, and the best matches replace them a moment later.

- Click a site for its details: open it, write yourself a note (notes are searchable too), or
  click **More like this** for sites that look alike.
- Narrow the wall with the colour, font and date filters under the search box, and choose how
  many sites per row on the right.
- Keyboard: **/** to search, arrow keys to move, **Enter** to open, **Esc** to clear or close.

**Help** on the new tab has all of this.

## What the labels on a site mean

- **Waiting to back up**: saved on this computer, and will back up once you're online.
- **Describing…**: being described so you can find it by description; it's already safe.
- **Failed** or **Couldn't describe**, with **Retry**: click Retry. It also retries by itself.

Nothing you save is lost if a step fails: the save stays on your computer until it's backed up.

## Getting your data out

**Export** on the new tab downloads everything (screenshots, notes and details) as one zip.

## Updating to a new version

Unzip the new version into the **same folder**, replacing the files. Then go to
`chrome://extensions` and click the reload icon on Visual Tabs. Your saves stay. (Loading it
from a different folder with **Load unpacked** also keeps them.)

## Something's wrong?

Send Akintayo a screenshot of the new tab and what you were doing. The text next to "Visual
Memory" at the top of the new tab says whether everything is backed up.
